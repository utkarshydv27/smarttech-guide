How to spot a suspicious message before you respond.

A quick, repeatable way to check unexpected texts, emails, calls and social-media messages.

Rule of thumb: Pause when a message creates urgency, asks for secrets or money, or pushes you to use a link you were not expecting.

1. Look for pressure

Messages that threaten to block an account, claim a parcel is stuck, promise a prize or demand action within minutes may be trying to make you act before you think. Real services may send legitimate alerts too, so pressure alone is a reason to verify—not proof by itself.

2. Protect credentials and payment information

Do not share OTPs, passwords, card PINs, CVV values or UPI PINs with a caller, message sender or an unverified website. If a message asks you to reveal a secret or install a remote-access application, stop and verify independently.

3. Inspect links safely

  • Do not open an unexpected link just to see where it goes.
  • Look carefully for misspellings, extra words, strange subdomains and shortened links.
  • Navigate to the service using a saved bookmark or by typing the known address yourself.
  • A padlock or HTTPS connection does not prove that a website is trustworthy.

4. Verify the request through another channel

Contact the bank, employer, courier or government department using contact information from its official website or your existing paperwork—not a number in the suspicious message. Ask a trusted person to review an unexpected payment or urgent request if you are unsure.

5. Check job, prize and investment offers

Be wary when a message promises guaranteed earnings, requires an upfront fee to get a job, asks you to move money for someone else, or promises unusually high returns with little or no risk. Independently verify the organisation and the terms before sharing documents or paying anything.

6. Use the local Safety Check

Our checker can highlight some common phrase patterns. It does not open or scan the link, identify the sender, contact an authority or make a definitive fraud determination. No warning found does not mean safe.

7. What if you already responded?

  • Stop communicating with the suspicious sender. Do not pay more money to “recover” funds.
  • If you shared account credentials, contact the provider through an official channel and change/revoke access as appropriate.
  • If money was transferred, contact your bank/payment provider promptly. In India, report suspected online financial fraud by calling 1930 promptly and use the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.
  • Preserve relevant messages, transaction references and screenshots for a report. Avoid posting sensitive details publicly.
