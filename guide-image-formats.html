JPG, PNG or WebP: which image format should you use?

Different formats serve different purposes. Consider image content, transparency needs, compatibility and the final destination.

JPEG / JPG

JPEG is a lossy format widely used for photographs. It can produce relatively small files, especially for images with natural colour and texture. Repeated lossy re-saving can reduce quality, and JPEG does not support transparency.

Consider JPEG for: photographs, everyday sharing and situations where compatibility is important.

PNG

PNG uses lossless compression and supports transparency. It is often suitable for interface assets, logos, diagrams and screenshots with sharp edges or text. However, a PNG photograph can be much larger than a lossy alternative.

Consider PNG for: transparent graphics, crisp screenshots and artwork that benefits from lossless storage.

WebP

WebP supports lossy and lossless compression, as well as transparency. It can be useful for web images where a balance between visual appearance and download size matters. The exact result depends on the source image and encoding settings.

Consider WebP for: website images and supported workflows where reducing transfer size is useful.

Quick comparison

How to choose

  1. For a photograph, compare JPEG and WebP output sizes at similar visual quality.
  2. For a transparent logo or graphic, use PNG or a supported WebP workflow.
  3. For text-heavy screenshots, check that small text and thin lines remain clear after conversion.
  4. Before uploading an image to a form or website, confirm that the destination accepts the format and file size.
